## Formula table

| Situation | Formula |
|-----------|---------|
| Compounded yearly | $A=P(1+r)^t$ |
| Compounded $n$ times per year | $A=P\left(1+\dfrac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$ |
| Interest earned | $I=A-P$ |

## Derivation (yearly)

After $1$ year: $A_1=P(1+r)$  
After $2$ years: $A_2=A_1(1+r)=P(1+r)^2$  
After $t$ years:

$$A=P(1+r)^t$$

Interest is added to the principal each period, unlike simple interest.

## Number examples

| $P$ | $r$ | $t$ | Amount $A$ | Interest $I$ |
|-----|-----|-----|------------|--------------|
| $\$2000$ | $4\%$ yearly | $3$ | $2000(1.04)^3=2249.73$ | $\$249.73$ |
| $\$5000$ | $3\%$ yearly | $2$ | $5000(1.03)^2=5304.50$ | $\$304.50$ |

Compare with simple interest on $\$2000$ at $4\%$ for $3$ years: $I=\$240$ (less than compound).
