## Formula table (plain text)

| Situation | Formula |
|-----------|---------|
| Compounded yearly | A = P x (1 + r)^t |
| Interest earned | I = A - P |

P = principal, r = yearly rate as a decimal, t = years.

## Why (yearly)

After 1 year: A = P x (1 + r)  
After 2 years: A = P x (1 + r) x (1 + r) = P x (1 + r)^2  
After t years: A = P x (1 + r)^t

Interest is added to the principal each year, unlike simple interest.

## Number examples

| P | r | t | Amount A | Interest I |
|---|---|---|----------|------------|
| 2000 | 0.04 | 3 | 2000 x (1.04)^3 = 2249.73 | 249.73 |
| 5000 | 0.03 | 2 | 5000 x (1.03)^2 = 5304.50 | 304.50 |

Compare with simple interest on 2000 at 4% for 3 years: I = 240 (less than compound).

## Remember

Compound interest grows faster because you earn interest on previous interest.
