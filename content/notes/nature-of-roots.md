## Formula table

| Discriminant $\Delta=b^2-4ac$ | Nature of roots |
|------------------------------|-----------------|
| $\Delta>0$ | two distinct real roots |
| $\Delta=0$ | one repeated real root |
| $\Delta<0$ | no real roots (complex conjugates) |

[[diagram:parabola|Shape of the quadratic helps interpret the roots]]

## Derivation

From the quadratic formula, the square root $\sqrt{\Delta}$ exists in the reals only when $\Delta\ge 0$.  
If $\Delta=0$, the $\pm$ gives the same value twice.

## Number examples

| Equation | $\Delta$ | Conclusion |
|----------|----------|------------|
| $x^2-5x+6=0$ | $25-24=1>0$ | two distinct real roots |
| $x^2+4x+4=0$ | $16-16=0$ | one repeated real root |
| $x^2+x+1=0$ | $1-4=-3<0$ | no real roots |
